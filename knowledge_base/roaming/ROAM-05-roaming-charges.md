# ROAM-05: Roaming Charges

## Category
Roaming

## Subcategory
Roaming Charges and Usage

## Problem

The customer reports unexpected, unclear, or disputed charges related to
using mobile services while roaming outside their home network or
country.

The issue may involve voice calls, SMS, mobile data, roaming packages,
usage allowances, or charges generated while connected to a foreign
partner network.

## Common Scenarios

- Customer received an unexpected roaming charge.
- Customer does not understand a roaming charge.
- Customer believes roaming should have been included in their plan.
- Customer exceeded a roaming data allowance.
- Customer was charged for roaming calls.
- Customer was charged for roaming SMS.
- Customer was charged after using mobile data abroad.
- Customer was charged despite believing roaming was disabled.
- Customer wants to know whether roaming usage will incur charges.
- Customer disputes a roaming charge.
- Customer reports charges after returning home from international travel.

## Possible Causes

- Roaming usage outside the customer's included allowance.
- Roaming package not active.
- Roaming package expired.
- Roaming usage exceeded an included allowance.
- Destination-specific roaming pricing.
- Voice call roaming charges.
- SMS roaming charges.
- Mobile data roaming charges.
- Background data usage while roaming.
- Automatic application synchronization.
- Data roaming remained enabled on the device.
- Usage occurred before a roaming package became active.
- Usage occurred after a roaming package expired.
- Charges were generated by a partner network.
- Delayed usage records were received from the partner network.
- Customer misunderstood the provider's roaming policy.
- Billing or usage-record discrepancy.

## Initial Diagnosis

Determine:

1. Customer's home country.
2. Roaming country or countries.
3. Date and time of roaming usage.
4. Type of usage.
5. Voice, SMS, or mobile data.
6. Amount of usage, when available.
7. Roaming plan or package active at the time.
8. Included roaming allowance, when applicable.
9. Whether the allowance was exceeded.
10. Whether the customer received a roaming notification.
11. Whether data roaming was enabled.
12. Whether the customer was connected to a supported partner network.
13. Whether the charge has already appeared on the bill.
14. Whether the charge is pending or finalized.
15. Whether the customer disputes the usage itself or only the amount
    charged.

## Roaming Charge Types

### Voice Calls

Charges may depend on:

- Destination
- Calling destination
- Duration
- Roaming partner
- Customer's plan
- Active roaming package

The system must use current provider-specific pricing information.

### SMS

Charges may depend on:

- Destination
- Number of messages
- Customer's plan
- Active roaming package
- Provider policy

### Mobile Data

Charges may depend on:

- Amount of data consumed
- Destination
- Roaming package
- Included allowance
- Provider pricing policy

Background data may consume roaming data even when the customer is not
actively browsing.

## Important Billing Distinction

The system should distinguish between:

`USAGE INFORMATION`

and:

`BILLING CHARGE`

Usage records describe activity.

Billing records determine the amount charged to the customer.

A usage record should not automatically be treated as a billing error.

## Initial Troubleshooting

### 1. Verify Roaming Destination

Confirm where the usage occurred.

Roaming pricing and eligibility may vary by country or region.

### 2. Verify Active Roaming Plan

Check whether a roaming package or feature was active during the usage
period.

Do not assume that activating a package after usage will retroactively
remove earlier charges.

### 3. Check Usage Records

Review available records for:

- Date
- Time
- Service
- Usage amount
- Destination
- Partner network
- Applicable roaming package

### 4. Check Included Allowance

Determine whether the customer's plan or package included:

- Voice
- SMS
- Data

Check whether the relevant allowance was exceeded.

### 5. Check Data Usage

For mobile data complaints, determine whether background applications,
automatic synchronization, updates, or other services may have consumed
data.

Do not assume the customer intentionally used all reported data.

### 6. Check Notifications

Determine whether the provider sent a roaming usage or charge
notification.

A notification may help explain when the customer became aware of the
usage, but its presence or absence does not by itself prove that a charge
is valid or invalid.

## Diagnosis Guidance

### Customer Says "I Was Not Using My Phone"

Check whether recorded data usage could have resulted from:

- Background applications
- Automatic updates
- Cloud synchronization
- Email synchronization
- Location services
- Other background network activity

Check actual usage records before concluding that the charge is valid or
invalid.

### Customer Says "Roaming Was Turned Off"

Verify:

- Device roaming settings
- Network usage records
- Account roaming status
- Time of usage

Device settings and account-level roaming controls are not necessarily
the same thing.

Do not conclude that a charge is incorrect solely from the customer's
device setting.

### Customer Says "Roaming Was Included"

Verify:

- Plan
- Active roaming package
- Destination
- Usage date
- Included allowance
- Usage amount

Included roaming may have destination, service, or usage limitations.

### Customer Says "I Was Charged After Buying a Roaming Package"

Check:

- Package activation time
- Usage time
- Package validity
- Usage before activation
- Usage after expiration

Do not automatically treat the charge as incorrect.

### Customer Disputes the Usage

Compare:

- Customer statement
- Usage records
- Destination
- Date/time
- Service type
- Partner-network records, when available

If the customer continues to dispute authenticated usage records,
escalate to the appropriate billing or roaming-support process.

### Customer Disputes the Amount

Verify:

- Applicable roaming rate
- Active package
- Included allowance
- Usage amount
- Destination
- Taxes or other applicable charges
- Provider billing policy

The AI must not calculate or promise a final adjustment unless the
required authoritative billing information is available.

## Resolution

The issue may be resolved by:

- Explaining the applicable roaming usage.
- Explaining included allowances.
- Explaining package validity.
- Identifying the service that generated the charge.
- Providing available usage records.
- Correcting a verified billing or provisioning error through an
  authorized process.
- Escalating a disputed charge for human billing review.

## Charge Dispute

When a customer disputes a roaming charge, the system should create a
structured review request containing:

- Charge date
- Charge type
- Roaming destination
- Usage amount
- Applicable package
- Usage record
- Customer's stated reason for dispute
- Relevant account information
- AI assessment
- Supporting evidence

The AI should not automatically approve or deny a billing dispute when
human review is required.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- The customer disputes authenticated roaming usage.
- The amount charged does not match available pricing information.
- The active roaming package appears to have been applied incorrectly.
- Usage occurred during a period when the customer was not expected to
  have roaming service.
- Duplicate charges are suspected.
- A billing-system error is suspected.
- Partner-network usage records conflict with provider billing records.
- The customer requests a charge adjustment that requires authorization.
- A large number of customers report similar unexpected roaming charges.
- Provider policy information is unavailable or contradictory.

## Potential Roaming Billing Incident

Multiple similar complaints may indicate a systemic issue.

Example:

Customer A
Destination: Spain
"Unexpected roaming data charge"

Customer B
Destination: Spain
"Roaming package was active but I was charged extra"

Customer C
Destination: Spain
"Same roaming charge appeared on my bill"

Same destination
+
Similar time period
+
Similar charge pattern
=
Potential roaming billing incident

The system should not declare a billing incident without authoritative
billing or provider confirmation.

## Human Agent Workflow

When a roaming-charge complaint is escalated:

1. Review the AI-generated summary.
2. Review the original customer complaint.
3. Verify the account and mobile line.
4. Verify roaming destination.
5. Review usage records.
6. Verify the applicable plan or roaming package.
7. Verify package activation and expiration times.
8. Check included allowances.
9. Verify applicable provider pricing.
10. Check whether charges are pending or finalized.
11. Review related complaints if a pattern is suspected.
12. Determine whether the charge appears valid, requires correction, or
    requires further investigation.
13. Apply authorized billing corrections when appropriate.
14. Escalate to billing or roaming specialists when required.
15. Record the final outcome.

- Date

- Charge date
- Charge type
- Usage amount
- Usage service
- Active roaming package

- Package expiration time
- Included allowance

Do not record unnecessary passwords, authentication codes, payment

## Agent Guidance

Do not invent roaming prices, allowances, taxes, or package terms.

Do not promise a refund or credit unless the provider's authorized
process allows it.

Do not automatically classify a roaming charge as fraudulent or
incorrect.

Do not assume that turning off data roaming removes all possible roaming
charges.

Do not assume that an included roaming package covers every service or
destination.

Do not expose internal billing systems or partner-network information.

Do not create duplicate incidents when an existing billing incident
already covers the same problem.

Provider-specific roaming prices, packages, allowances, taxes, billing
rules, usage-record policies, dispute procedures, and credit/refund
authorization should be maintained separately as provider-specific
policy documents.

## Scalability Requirements

For a large network provider, roaming-charge complaints should be
processed using automated billing and usage analysis.

The architecture should support:

- Charge classification
- Usage-record matching
- Destination-based clustering
- Package matching
- Allowance comparison
- Time-window analysis
- Complaint similarity analysis
- Duplicate charge detection
- Potential billing-incident detection
- Related-ticket association
- Configurable thresholds
- Human confirmation
- Billing-team escalation
- Large numbers of simultaneous complaints

The system should allow large numbers of roaming billing complaints to
be analyzed without requiring agents to manually compare every ticket.

"My roaming package included data, but I was charged for data during
the package validity period."

Available information:

Package:
Active

Included Data:
5 GB

Recorded Usage:
1.5 GB

Applicable Destination:
Supported

Additional roaming data fee

billing.

AI assessment:

POTENTIAL BILLING DISCREPANCY

Human action:

Review provider billing records, package rules, and applicable pricing
before approving or rejecting an adjustment.
