# CS-02: Complaint Handling and Escalation

## Category

customer_service

## Subcategory

Complaint Handling and Escalation

## Problem

The customer has an unresolved complaint, is dissatisfied with a previous support interaction, or requests escalation because the issue has not been resolved through normal support.

The complaint may involve repeated contacts, unsuccessful troubleshooting, delayed resolution, or dissatisfaction with the service received.

## Example Customer Complaints

- "I already contacted support but nobody fixed my issue."
- "I have complained several times and the problem is still not resolved."
- "I want to escalate this complaint."
- "Your support team has not helped me."
- "I was told this would be fixed, but nothing has happened."
- "I want to speak to a supervisor."
- "This is my third time contacting support about the same issue."

## AI Assessment

UNRESOLVED CUSTOMER COMPLAINT

The customer indicates that normal support has not resolved the issue or that escalation is required.

## Recommended Human Action

Review the customer's previous support interactions and identify the unresolved issue.

Verify:

- Previous complaint or case reference.
- Date and time of previous contacts.
- Actions already performed.
- Troubleshooting already completed.
- Current status of the issue.
- Any commitments or resolution timelines previously provided.
- Whether the issue is already assigned to a specialist team.

Avoid repeating troubleshooting steps that have already been completed unless there is a specific reason to do so.

## Escalation Criteria

Escalate when:

- The issue requires a specialist team.
- A previously promised resolution has not occurred.
- The customer requests supervisor-level assistance.
- The issue involves multiple unresolved services.
- The issue requires account-level investigation.
- A technical or operational team must intervene.
- The complaint involves a significant service impact.

## Priority Considerations

Higher priority may be appropriate when:

- Multiple services are unavailable.
- The issue affects business-critical service.
- The customer has experienced prolonged service disruption.
- The customer has repeatedly contacted support without resolution.
- There is a suspected widespread service issue.
- A previous escalation has failed to resolve the problem.

## Human Action

The support agent should:

1. Acknowledge the unresolved complaint.
2. Review previous case information.
3. Confirm the current issue with the customer.
4. Avoid unnecessary repetition.
5. Determine the appropriate escalation path.
6. Record the escalation and relevant details.
7. Provide the customer with the applicable case or escalation reference.

## Important Notes

Do not promise a specific resolution time unless an authorized service timeline is available.

Do not claim that an escalation guarantees a particular outcome.

Do not repeatedly route the customer through basic troubleshooting when the issue has already been investigated.

## Source Basis

This knowledge article is based on standardized telecom customer-support escalation workflows for handling unresolved complaints, reviewing previous support activity, avoiding repeated troubleshooting, determining escalation priority, and routing cases to appropriate specialist or supervisory teams.
