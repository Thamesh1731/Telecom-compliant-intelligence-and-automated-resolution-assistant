# BILL-01: Incorrect or Unexpected Bill Amount

## Category
Billing

## Problem
The customer believes that the amount shown on their telecom bill is
incorrect or different from what they expected.

## Common Symptoms
- Total bill is higher than expected
- Monthly charge has changed unexpectedly
- Customer was charged more than the advertised or expected rate
- A previous discount or credit is no longer visible
- Customer sees a charge they do not understand
- A plan or service change resulted in an unexpected amount
- Taxes, fees, or surcharges increased the total
- Partial-month charges appear on the bill
- A previous adjustment or credit has not appeared yet

## Possible Causes
- Plan or service change
- New service or equipment
- Partial-month or prorated charges
- Promotional discount or credit expired
- Taxes or regulatory fees
- Additional usage or overage
- Late payment fee
- Service restoration fee
- Previous credit or adjustment has not yet been applied
- Account or billing error
- Unauthorized charge

## Initial Diagnosis

Before treating the bill as an error:

1. Compare the current bill with the previous bill.
2. Identify which individual charge or charges changed.
3. Check whether the customer's plan or services were recently changed.
4. Check whether a promotional discount or credit ended.
5. Check for partial-month or prorated charges.
6. Check for taxes, regulatory fees, or other applicable charges.
7. Check for equipment, installation, usage, or other one-time charges.
8. Check whether a previous credit or adjustment is still pending.
9. Determine whether the customer recognizes the service or transaction
   associated with the disputed charge.

## Troubleshooting Procedure

### Step 1: Identify the disputed amount

Determine:

- Amount in question
- Date of the charge
- Billing period
- Description shown on the bill

### Step 2: Compare with previous billing

Compare the disputed charge with previous bills.

Determine whether:

- The charge is new
- The charge increased
- A previous credit disappeared
- The customer's recurring charge changed

### Step 3: Check account changes

Review recent:

- Plan changes
- Service additions or removals
- Equipment purchases
- Installation activity
- Account changes
- Promotional offers
- Discount changes

### Step 4: Check applicable fees and taxes

Determine whether the difference is caused by:

- Taxes
- Regulatory fees
- Surcharges
- Other applicable service fees

Taxes and fees can vary according to the customer's service and
location.

### Step 5: Check credits and adjustments

Determine whether the customer was promised a credit or adjustment and
whether it has already been applied.

Some credits or adjustments may appear on a later bill depending on the
provider's billing process.

### Step 6: Determine whether the charge is unauthorized

If the customer states that they did not authorize the service,
purchase, or charge, treat it as a potential unauthorized-charge
complaint rather than simply an incorrect bill.

## Resolution

If the charge is valid and can be explained:

1. Explain the reason for the charge clearly.
2. Identify the relevant billing period.
3. Explain any applicable plan change, fee, tax, credit, or adjustment.
4. Confirm the expected amount on future bills when the information is
   available.

If the charge is determined to be incorrect:

1. Record the disputed amount.
2. Submit the appropriate billing correction or dispute.
3. Apply an eligible credit or adjustment according to the provider's
   policy.
4. Inform the customer of the expected resolution process.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- The charge cannot be explained using available account information.
- The customer disputes the validity or amount of the charge.
- A billing correction is required but cannot be completed automatically.
- A promised credit or adjustment is missing.
- The customer repeatedly reports the same unresolved billing problem.
- The account contains conflicting billing information.
- The issue requires a billing specialist or manual account review.

## Agent Information to Record

Record:

- Ticket ID
- Billing period
- Disputed amount
- Charge description
- Date of charge
- Previous bill amount
- Current bill amount
- Relevant plan or account changes
- Applicable credits or discounts
- Customer explanation
- Action taken

## Agent Guidance

Do not assume that a higher bill is necessarily a billing error.

First identify the exact difference between the expected and actual
amount.

Do not promise a credit or refund unless the applicable billing process
confirms that the customer is eligible.

If the customer reports an unauthorized charge, route the complaint
through the appropriate unauthorized-charge or billing-dispute process.
