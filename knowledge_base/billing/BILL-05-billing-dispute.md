# BILL-05: Billing Dispute

## Category
Billing

## Subcategory
Billing Dispute

## Problem
The customer formally disputes a charge, balance, fee, adjustment,
or other billing decision and believes the issue has not been
satisfactorily resolved through normal billing support.

## Common Scenarios

- Customer disputes a charge after receiving an explanation
- Customer believes the provider's billing calculation is incorrect
- A promised billing correction was not applied
- A disputed charge remains on the account
- A billing credit was not applied
- Customer disputes a fee or surcharge
- Customer disputes a service or equipment charge
- Customer repeatedly reports the same unresolved billing problem

## Distinguishing a Billing Dispute

Use this category when the customer is actively challenging the validity
or resolution of a billing issue.

Do not classify a complaint as a formal billing dispute merely because
the customer asks why their bill changed.

Examples:

"Why is my bill $20 higher this month?"
→ BILL-01: Incorrect or Unexpected Bill

"I don't recognize this charge."
→ BILL-02: Unauthorized or Unrecognized Charge

"I paid my bill but the payment isn't showing."
→ BILL-03: Payment Problem

"I was promised a credit but haven't received it."
→ BILL-04: Refund or Account Credit

"I already contacted support about this charge and they refused to
correct it. I want to dispute the charge."
→ BILL-05: Billing Dispute

## Initial Diagnosis

Before escalating a billing dispute:

1. Identify the disputed charge or balance.
2. Determine the billing period.
3. Review the customer's previous interactions about the issue.
4. Check whether the provider has already investigated the complaint.
5. Check whether a correction, credit, or refund was previously
   promised.
6. Determine whether the customer has provided supporting information.
7. Check whether the issue is actually an unauthorized charge,
   payment problem, or refund issue that should use another billing
   workflow.

## Information Required

Collect, when available:

- Ticket ID
- Account identifier
- Billing period
- Disputed amount
- Charge description
- Date of charge
- Customer's explanation
- Previous support interactions
- Previous case or ticket references
- Promised credits or adjustments
- Supporting documentation
- Customer's requested resolution

## Supporting Evidence

Useful evidence may include:

- Previous bills
- Payment confirmations
- Account statements
- Provider messages
- Email confirmations
- Text confirmations
- Service agreements
- Promotion or offer documentation
- Previous support case information

Do not request or store unnecessary sensitive information.

Sensitive financial credentials such as full card numbers, bank
passwords, or security credentials should not be included in the
complaint record.

## Dispute Handling Procedure

### Step 1: Identify the Dispute

Determine exactly what the customer is challenging.

Examples:

- Charge amount
- Service charge
- Equipment charge
- Fee
- Tax or surcharge
- Promotional adjustment
- Account balance
- Previous billing decision

### Step 2: Review Account History

Check the available account and billing records.

Determine whether the disputed charge can be explained by:

- Plan changes
- Service changes
- Equipment
- Promotions
- Credits
- Payments
- Fees
- Taxes
- Previous adjustments

### Step 3: Review Previous Resolution Attempts

Determine whether the customer has already contacted support.

If so, review:

- Previous ticket
- Previous agent response
- Resolution offered
- Promised correction
- Credit or refund status

### Step 4: Determine the Appropriate Action

If the issue can be resolved using available information, provide the
customer with the explanation or correction.

If the dispute requires manual investigation, escalate it to the
appropriate billing support team.

## Resolution

A billing dispute may be resolved by:

- Explaining the disputed charge
- Correcting an incorrect charge
- Applying an eligible credit
- Processing an eligible refund
- Correcting an account balance
- Providing additional billing documentation
- Escalating the case for specialist review

The exact resolution depends on the provider's billing policies and
the findings of the investigation.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- The customer formally disputes a charge after normal explanation.
- The charge cannot be verified using available records.
- Previous support failed to resolve the complaint.
- A promised correction, credit, or refund was not completed.
- Manual billing investigation is required.
- The customer provides evidence contradicting the current billing
  record.
- The dispute involves multiple billing periods.
- The issue involves suspected unauthorized activity or fraud.
- The customer repeatedly reports the same unresolved billing dispute.

## Agent Guidance

### Do not automatically side with the customer

A disputed charge is not automatically an incorrect charge.

Review the available account information before recommending a credit,
refund, or correction.

### Do not automatically reject the dispute

If the available records cannot establish why a charge occurred,
escalate for appropriate investigation rather than inventing an
explanation.

### Do not promise an outcome

Do not promise a refund, credit, cancellation, or correction until the
applicable provider policy and account records confirm eligibility.

### Do not invent deadlines

Dispute deadlines and resolution times vary by provider and issue type.
Use provider-specific policy information when available.

## Agent Information to Record

Record:

- Disputed amount
- Billing period
- Charge description
- Reason for dispute
- Previous support interactions
- Previous resolution attempts
- Evidence provided
- Account findings
- Action taken
- Escalation reason
- Requested customer resolution

## Provider Escalation

If the dispute cannot be resolved by the current support agent,
forward it to the appropriate billing specialist or escalation queue.

The escalation record should contain the complete investigation context
so that the next agent does not need to repeat the customer's entire
history.

## External Regulatory Escalation

If the customer's issue remains unresolved after the provider's
applicable complaint process, the customer may have additional
regulatory or dispute-resolution options.

For US telecommunications complaints, the FCC accepts complaints
concerning billing and service issues. The FCC encourages consumers to
contact their provider first and may serve qualifying complaints on the
provider. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The AI should not present regulatory escalation as the default solution.
It should first follow the provider's internal support and dispute
process.
