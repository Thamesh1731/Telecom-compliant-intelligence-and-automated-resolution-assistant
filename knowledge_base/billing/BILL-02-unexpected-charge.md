# BILL-02: Unauthorized or Unrecognized Charge

## Category
Billing

## Subcategory
Unauthorized Charge

## Problem
The customer reports a charge on their telecom bill that they do not
recognize or did not authorize.

## Common Symptoms

- Customer does not recognize a charge
- Customer says they did not order the associated service
- An unfamiliar product or service appears on the bill
- An unfamiliar equipment charge appears
- An unfamiliar third-party charge appears
- Customer reports an account or service change they did not authorize

## Possible Causes

- Service or product approved by another authorized account user
- Newly added service or feature
- Equipment purchase or change
- Third-party purchase or subscription
- Account change
- Billing error
- Unauthorized account activity
- Fraudulent account or service change

## Initial Diagnosis

Do not immediately assume that an unfamiliar charge is fraudulent.

First determine:

1. The exact charge amount.
2. The date the charge appeared.
3. The description of the charge.
4. The billing period.
5. Whether another authorized user has access to the account.
6. Whether a related service, product, equipment, or subscription was
   recently added.
7. Whether an account confirmation, email, text message, or other
   authorization record exists.

## Verification Procedure

### Step 1: Identify the charge

Record:

- Charge description
- Amount
- Date
- Billing period
- Associated service or product, if shown

### Step 2: Check account history

Check whether the account contains:

- Recent service changes
- New products
- New equipment
- Added features
- Third-party purchases
- Subscription changes

### Step 3: Check authorization

Determine whether the charge was approved by:

- The customer
- Another authorized account user
- An account representative or approved account action

Where available, review relevant confirmation records such as:

- Email confirmation
- Text confirmation
- Online confirmation
- Account activity

### Step 4: Determine the complaint type

If the charge is valid and authorized:

Explain the charge and its relationship to the customer's account.

If authorization cannot be established:

Treat the charge as a potential unauthorized-charge dispute.

If the customer reports that their identity or account was used without
permission:

Treat the issue as a potential fraud or unauthorized-account case.

## Resolution

### If the charge is authorized

Explain:

- What the charge represents
- When it was added
- Which service, product, or account action generated it

No credit should be promised unless the customer is eligible under the
applicable billing policy.

### If the charge cannot be verified

Create or submit a billing dispute according to the provider's dispute
process.

Record the disputed charge and supporting information.

### If fraud is suspected

Route the case through the appropriate fraud or unauthorized-account
process.

The customer may require additional identity or account verification.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- Authorization for the charge cannot be established.
- The customer formally disputes the charge.
- The charge involves suspected fraud.
- An account or service was changed without customer authorization.
- A third-party charge cannot be identified or resolved.
- A billing credit or correction requires manual review.
- The customer has repeatedly reported the same unresolved charge.

## Agent Information to Record

Record:

- Ticket ID
- Charge amount
- Charge description
- Charge date
- Billing period
- Associated service or product
- Account changes related to the charge
- Authorization evidence, when available
- Customer's explanation
- Whether the charge is disputed
- Action taken
- Escalation reason, if applicable

## Agent Guidance

Do not tell the customer that a charge is fraudulent without evidence.

First determine whether the charge was authorized by the customer or
another authorized account user.

Do not promise a refund or credit before the applicable dispute or fraud
review has been completed.

If the customer reports unauthorized account creation or unauthorized
account changes, route the complaint through the appropriate fraud
process rather than treating it as an ordinary billing explanation.

## Source Basis

This article is a normalized knowledge article based on publicly
available guidance from:

- Federal Communications Commission (FCC)
- AT&T public billing and fraud support documentation
- Xfinity public billing dispute guidance

The FCC distinguishes unauthorized telecom charges ("cramming") from
ordinary billing complaints. Xfinity's current dispute process similarly
checks account records and authorization evidence before determining
whether a disputed charge may receive a credit. AT&T provides separate
processes for unauthorized accounts and account changes.

Provider-specific dispute deadlines and contact information have been
excluded from this provider-neutral article.
