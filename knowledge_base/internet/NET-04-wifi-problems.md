# NET-04: Wi-Fi Connection Problems

## Category
Internet / Network

## Subcategory
Wi-Fi

## Problem
The customer reports that their Wi-Fi connection is unavailable,
weak, slow, unstable, or cannot connect to a device.

## Common Symptoms
- Device cannot connect to Wi-Fi
- Wi-Fi signal is weak
- Wi-Fi becomes slow at greater distances from the router
- Connection drops in certain areas of the home
- Some devices connect while others cannot
- Wi-Fi works near the router but poorly in other areas
- Wi-Fi performance changes depending on location
- Wi-Fi connection is affected by interference

## Possible Causes
- Device is too far from the router
- Walls or other physical obstructions weaken the signal
- Electronic devices cause wireless interference
- Router is poorly positioned
- Too many devices are using the wireless network
- High-bandwidth activities are consuming available wireless capacity
- Router or gateway problem
- Device-specific Wi-Fi problem
- Wireless configuration problem
- Wi-Fi coverage does not adequately reach the affected area

## Initial Diagnosis

Determine whether:

1. The problem affects one device or multiple devices.
2. The problem occurs only in a specific location.
3. The Wi-Fi connection improves when the device is moved closer to
   the router.
4. Other devices can connect normally.
5. Internet access works through a wired connection, if available.

## Troubleshooting Procedure

### 1. Check Wi-Fi on the affected device

Verify that Wi-Fi is enabled and that the device is connected to the
correct wireless network.

If the device cannot connect, verify that the correct Wi-Fi credentials
are being used.

### 2. Move closer to the router

Move the affected device closer to the router and test the connection.

If performance improves significantly, the issue may be related to
Wi-Fi range or signal strength.

### 3. Check router placement

The router should be positioned in a relatively central and open
location.

Avoid placing the router:

- Inside cabinets or enclosed spaces
- Behind large objects
- Near large metal objects
- Near devices that may cause wireless interference
- In locations separated from devices by unnecessary walls or
  obstructions

### 4. Check for wireless interference

Potential sources of interference include:

- Other wireless networks
- Bluetooth or wireless transmitting devices
- Microwave ovens
- Cordless phones
- Other electronic equipment

Move the router or affected device away from significant sources of
interference where practical.

### 5. Check connected devices

Determine whether many devices are simultaneously using the Wi-Fi network.

Temporarily disconnect devices that are not required, particularly
devices performing high-bandwidth activities such as streaming or large
downloads.

### 6. Test another device

Connect another device to the same Wi-Fi network.

If another device works normally, investigate the original device.

If multiple devices experience the same problem, investigate the
Wi-Fi network or router.

### 7. Restart the router

Restart the router or gateway and allow it to reconnect completely.

Test the Wi-Fi connection again after the restart.

### 8. Test from different locations

Test the affected device at different distances from the router.

Record whether:

- The connection works near the router
- The connection becomes weak farther away
- Certain rooms or areas consistently have poor coverage

## Diagnosis Guidance

### Only one device is affected

The problem may be related to:

- The device's Wi-Fi settings
- Device hardware
- Device-specific network configuration

### Multiple devices are affected

The problem is more likely related to:

- Router or gateway
- Wi-Fi configuration
- Wireless interference
- Coverage
- Network equipment

### Wi-Fi works near the router but not farther away

The problem is likely related to:

- Signal strength
- Distance
- Physical obstructions
- Router placement
- Wi-Fi coverage

### Wi-Fi is poor but wired Internet works normally

The Internet service itself may be functioning correctly.

Investigate the wireless network, router placement, interference, or
Wi-Fi coverage before treating the problem as an Internet service outage.

## Resolution

If the issue is caused by device position, router placement, interference,
or excessive wireless activity, provide the appropriate troubleshooting
guidance.

If the issue is isolated to one device, guide the customer through
device-specific Wi-Fi troubleshooting.

If the issue affects multiple devices and persists after standard
troubleshooting, investigate the router, gateway, service, or network.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- Multiple devices continue to experience Wi-Fi problems after standard troubleshooting.
- Wi-Fi remains unstable after restarting the router. 
- Wired and wireless connections both experience problems.
- The customer reports persistent connectivity problems that cannot be
  resolved through standard troubleshooting.
- The same Wi-Fi problem has been repeatedly reported.
- A provider-side network problem is suspected.

## Agent Information to Record

Before escalation, record:

- Number of affected devices
- Device types
- Approximate distance from router
- Whether the problem occurs in specific areas
- Whether moving closer improves the connection
- Whether other devices work normally
- Whether wired connectivity works, if available
- Whether restarting the router helped
- Whether interference or excessive network activity was identified

## Agent Guidance

Do not immediately classify every Wi-Fi complaint as an Internet
service outage.

First determine whether the Internet service works through another
device or wired connection, when available.

A Wi-Fi coverage problem and an Internet service outage are different
problems and should be handled accordingly.
