# NET-06: Router, Modem, and Gateway Problems

## Category
Internet / Network

## Subcategory
Router / Modem / Gateway / Equipment

## Problem
The customer reports that their router, modem, gateway, or related
network equipment is not working correctly or may be causing Internet
connectivity problems.

## Common Symptoms
- Router or modem has no power
- Router or modem shows an abnormal status indicator
- Internet status indicator shows no connection
- Devices cannot connect to the Internet
- Wi-Fi stops working
- Connection repeatedly drops
- Network equipment restarts unexpectedly
- Internet works intermittently
- Equipment becomes unresponsive

## Possible Causes
- Power connection problem
- Loose or disconnected cable
- Router or modem malfunction
- Gateway connection problem
- Network-side connection failure
- Damaged cable
- Equipment configuration problem
- Equipment overheating or instability
- Provider-side service problem

## Initial Diagnosis

Determine whether the problem is caused by the equipment itself or by
the Internet service.

Check:

1. Whether the router, modem, or gateway has power.
2. Whether the equipment's status indicators show an abnormal condition.
3. Whether power and network cables are securely connected.
4. Whether the Internet connection is unavailable on multiple devices.
5. Whether a service outage is currently reported.
6. Whether restarting the equipment resolves the problem.

## Troubleshooting Procedure

### 1. Check Power

Verify that the router, modem, or gateway is connected to a working
power source and that the device is powered on.

If the device has no power indicator, check the power connection and
power supply.

### 2. Check Network Connections

Verify that required cables are securely connected to the appropriate
ports.

Check both ends of each relevant cable.

Do not force connectors or use tools unless the equipment documentation
specifically requires them.

### 3. Check Status Indicators

Check the equipment's status lights or indicators.

The meaning of indicators varies by equipment model, but they may provide
information about:

- Power
- Internet/WAN connection
- Broadband connection
- Ethernet connection
- Wi-Fi status

Use the equipment or provider documentation to interpret a specific
indicator.

### 4. Restart the Equipment

Restart the router, modem, or gateway using the provider's recommended
restart procedure.

After restarting:

1. Wait for the equipment to complete its startup process.
2. Wait for the relevant connection indicators to stabilize.
3. Test the Internet connection again.

### 5. Test Multiple Devices

Test the Internet connection using another device when possible.

If multiple devices cannot access the Internet, the problem is more
likely related to the router, gateway, service, or network.

If only one device is affected, investigate that device separately.

### 6. Check for a Service Outage

Before performing extensive equipment troubleshooting, check whether
the provider reports an Internet or network outage affecting the
customer's location.

A confirmed provider-side outage should not be treated as a simple
router failure.

## Diagnosis Guidance

### Equipment has no power

Possible causes:

- Power cable disconnected
- Power supply problem
- Electrical outlet problem
- Equipment failure

### Equipment is powered but Internet is unavailable

Possible causes:

- Provider connection problem
- Service outage
- WAN/broadband connection problem
- Router or modem configuration problem
- Equipment failure

### Status indicator shows a connection failure

Check the equipment documentation to determine what the indicator means.

Then:

1. Verify cable connections.
2. Check for an outage.
3. Restart the equipment.
4. Test the connection again.

### Multiple devices cannot connect

Investigate:

- Router/gateway
- Internet service
- Network connection
- Service outage

### Only one device cannot connect

The router or Internet service may be functioning correctly.

Investigate the affected device's Wi-Fi or network configuration.

## Resolution

If the problem is caused by a loose connection, reconnect the cable
securely and test the service.

If restarting the equipment restores connectivity, confirm that the
Internet connection is stable.

If the equipment continues to show abnormal behavior or the Internet
connection remains unavailable, technical investigation may be required.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- Equipment remains unresponsive after the recommended restart
  procedure.
- Equipment repeatedly loses connectivity.
- Status indicators continue to show a persistent connection failure.
- Required cables and power connections have been verified.
- Multiple devices remain unable to access the Internet.
- A provider-side network problem is suspected.
- The equipment appears physically damaged or defective.
- The customer repeatedly experiences unresolved equipment-related
  connectivity problems.

## Agent Information to Record

Before escalation, record:

- Equipment type, when available
- Equipment status indicators
- Power status
- Cable connection status
- Whether the equipment was restarted
- Result after restart
- Number of affected devices
- Whether Wi-Fi or wired connections are affected
- Whether an outage was identified
- Whether the issue is persistent or intermittent

## Agent Guidance

Do not recommend a factory reset as a routine first troubleshooting
step.

A factory reset can remove customer configuration and should only be
performed when appropriate for the specific equipment and support
procedure.

Do not assume that abnormal router behavior means the router itself is
defective. A provider-side service problem can produce similar symptoms.
