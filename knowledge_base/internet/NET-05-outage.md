# NET-05: Internet Service Outage

## Category
Internet / Network

## Subcategory
Service Outage

## Problem
The customer reports a complete or widespread loss of Internet service
that may be caused by a provider-side network outage.

## Common Symptoms
- Internet service is unavailable
- Multiple devices cannot access the Internet
- Multiple customers in the same area may be affected
- Service was working previously and suddenly stopped
- Router or gateway may remain powered while Internet access is unavailable
- Service may be restored without changes to the customer's equipment

## Possible Causes
- Provider network outage
- Local network infrastructure failure
- Fiber or cable infrastructure problem
- Power-related network disruption
- Maintenance activity
- Damage to network infrastructure
- Regional service disruption
- Equipment failure affecting a larger network segment

## Initial Diagnosis

Determine whether the problem is likely isolated to the customer or
affects a broader service area.

Check:

1. Whether multiple devices at the customer's location are affected.
2. Whether the router or gateway remains powered.
3. Whether a provider outage or service-status notification exists.
4. Whether other customers in the same geographic area are reporting
   similar problems, when such information is available.
5. Whether the problem began suddenly after previously working normally.
6. Whether standard local troubleshooting has already been completed.

## Outage Verification

If the provider has an outage or service-status system, check whether
the customer's location is included in a known outage.

An outage notification may provide:

- Affected service
- Geographic area
- Current outage status
- Estimated restoration information, when available
- Repair or restoration updates

Do not assume that the absence of an outage notification proves that
there is no provider-side problem.

## Troubleshooting During a Suspected Outage

1. Confirm that the customer's router or gateway has power.
2. Confirm that necessary cables remain connected.
3. Determine whether multiple devices are affected.
4. Check the provider's current service-status or outage information.
5. If no known outage is found, perform standard Internet connectivity
   troubleshooting.
6. If the issue continues and local equipment appears functional,
   escalate for technical investigation.

## Resolution

If a confirmed outage exists:

- Inform the customer that the service disruption has been identified.
- Provide the available outage status or restoration information.
- Avoid unnecessary repeated troubleshooting when the provider has
  already confirmed a network outage.
- Monitor the case according to the provider's support process.

If the outage is resolved, verify that the customer's service has
returned.

If service does not return after the reported outage is resolved,
perform additional troubleshooting and escalate if necessary.

## Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

- A provider-side outage is suspected but no outage information is available.
- The customer remains without service after a reported outage has been resolved.
- Multiple devices remain disconnected after local troubleshooting.
- The issue appears to affect multiple customers or locations.
- The customer's equipment appears functional but Internet service remains unavailable.
- The issue is persistent or repeatedly reported.
- Technical investigation is required.

## Priority Guidance

A suspected widespread outage may require higher operational priority
than an isolated customer-side connectivity problem because multiple
customers may be affected.

Priority should consider:

- Number of affected customers, when known
- Geographic scope
- Duration
- Services affected
- Repeated reports
- Impact on critical services

## Agent Information to Record

When handling a suspected outage, record:

- Customer city and state
- ZIP code
- Time the service stopped working
- Services affected
- Number of affected devices
- Router/gateway status
- Whether an official outage has been identified
- Outage status or reference information, when available
- Whether service returned after the outage was resolved

## Agent Guidance

Do not treat every "no Internet" complaint as an outage.

Use the available evidence to distinguish between:

- Customer device problem
- Wi-Fi problem
- Router/equipment problem
- Individual connectivity problem
- Provider-side outage

Do not promise a restoration time unless an authoritative provider
source has supplied one.
